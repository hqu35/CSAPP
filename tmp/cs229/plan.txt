Version legend
• A18-Ln = Stanford CS229 Autumn 2018, Lecture n (Andrew Ng era/classical presentation).
• S20-Ln = Stanford CS229 Summer 2020, Lecture n (used selectively for explicit theory review).
• F25-Ln = Stanford CS229 Fall 2025, Lecture n (modern course spine).
• F25 TA-Ln = Fall 2025 course-assistant lecture n.
• PSet 0-4 = the Stanford-style problem-set sequence. If your source version numbers problems differently, map by topic;
 the completion date in this plan stays fixed.
• Lab 1-7 = personalized implementation labs in this plan, not necessarily official Stanford lab titles.

Non-negotiable execution rule
Lecture/Core -> Math Patch -> Practice/PSet/Lab -> Mastery/Stop Condition. Do not postpone the ML topic
until you have "finished" an entire prerequisite subject. Patch only the math actually blocking the current
lecture.

December 20 final mastery questions
1. What is a loss/objective function, and how is empirical risk related to training data?
2. Why is the negative gradient a local descent direction, and what extra assumptions are needed for convergence?
3. How do linear regression, logistic regression, and GLMs relate?
4. What is likelihood, and how does MLE turn a probabilistic model into an optimization problem?
5. What is the difference between generative and discriminative learning?
6. What problem does regularization solve, and how does it interact with bias and variance?
7. What is the geometric idea behind SVMs and the computational idea behind kernels?
8. How do K-means, GMMs, and EM relate?
9. What is backpropagation mathematically?
10. Why does PCA reduce dimension using eigenvectors / singular vectors?
11. How does the Bellman equation lead to value iteration and policy iteration?
12. How do classical ideas such as likelihood, representation, optimization, and generalization reappear in transformers
 and LLM training?
